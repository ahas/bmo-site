## How notes and artifacts differ

| | Notes | Artifacts |
|---|---|---|
| Who writes them | You write and edit them yourself | Mostly made and kept by BMO or research |
| What they are | Documents you keep editing | Organised records, research results, learning materials, saved web pages |
| Sharing | A link, or read or edit access for people in your company | A link, people in your company, or read access for a whole organisation |

## Notes

Open them with "Notes" under "Applications" in the sidebar.

### The list

The list on the left has "Bookmarks", "My notes", "Shared with me" and, at the very bottom, "Trash". In "My notes" you can make folders up to eight levels deep with "New folder". Folders are your own way of organising, so they are never shared with anyone. Deleting a folder doesn't delete the notes in it; they move up one level.

### Writing

Click "New note" and write a title and body. The body can hold headings, lists, to-do lists, quotes, code blocks, tables and images, and each note can have one emoji icon.

- Notes save themselves as you type, with "Saving…" and "Saved" shown at the top.
- Only one person edits a note at a time. If it was saved somewhere else first, you are told "This note was saved from elsewhere." and asked to reload it.
- Ctrl+Z undoes, and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes.
- Type `[[` or `@` to pick another note and link to it. Below a note you see the notes that link to it.

### Bookmarks and comments

- You can bookmark notes, folders and a single line of a note ("Bookmark line"). A line bookmark follows the line even if it moves or its text changes.
- Select some text and add a comment, and a thread opens below that line. A thread can be folded away as resolved and opened again.

### Finding and sharing

Ctrl+K opens the search window. It searches titles and bodies, and you can choose the sort order (best match, recently edited, date created) and filters (titles only, my notes, shared with me, trash, date edited).

"Share" has two parts.

- **Link:** a read-only link that opens for a set period, or with no time limit. It can be viewed without signing in.
- **People in your company:** give people in the same company read or edit access. With edit access they can also comment.

Only the note's owner can use the trash, delete for good, or change the sharing itself.

### Writing with BMO in a note

- **"Agent":** select some text and click "Agent" for a menu with polish, fix spelling and grammar, make longer or shorter, change tone, translate into Korean, English or Japanese, continue writing, summarise, outline and more. You can also ask in your own words. The result is shown below first, and you choose to replace, insert below, ask again or discard.
- **"Talk":** opens a panel beside the note to talk about the whole note with your BMO's note assistant. It edits only the parts that changed into the note, and if it can't, it says why in one line. "Apply all" overwrites the whole note text with the answer.

Either way, a single undo reverts what BMO changed.

### What notes don't have

Notes have no version history, no simultaneous editing by several people and no cover image. Text inside images is not searched.

## Artifacts

"Artifacts" under "Applications" in the sidebar is where the documents BMO keeps for you are gathered. Say "write this up as a document" in a room, and BMO or research leaves the result here and hands you a link.

### Kinds of document

| Kind | Contents |
|---|---|
| "Research" | What research found: the key answer, a three-line summary and the pages it rests on |
| "Record" | Material that has been organised and kept |
| "Learning material" | Learning material bound like a book, in parts, chapters and sections |
| "Original" | A web page saved whole |
| "Page" | An HTML page kept as it is |

### The front page and the list

The front page has "Newest", groups by kind and, in a side column on wide screens, "Kinds", "Recently read" and "Most read" ("Last 7 days"). Type a search and it turns into a list, which you can filter by kind. You can also find a document by its number (such as `#31`).

### Reading a document

Open a document to see its body, "Contents", "Referred pages" and attachments. Documents with a read-aloud voice have "Listen while you read". At the top right are "Translation" (when the document is written in a language other than yours), "Instruction", "Download", "Share" and document info.

### Who can read it

- At first a document is read by its owner only. Even a head of the same organisation can't read it until it is shared. The platform administrator can read every document but doesn't change other people's documents.
- "Share" offers three ways: a link (with a set period or none), people in the same company, and "Share with units", for an organisation and everyone under it. Sharing with company people and organisations is read-only.
- In the list, documents with an open link show "Public" or "Public · until a date", and the rest "Private".

Where document files are kept is decided by the "Artifact storage" tab on the organisation screen.

## Good to know

- A document's share link with no set period keeps opening even when the service restarts or a new version is deployed.
- An expired link keeps only what it used to open, and doesn't show the body.
- If the owner leaves the company, people a document was shared with can still read it, and documents that weren't shared are locked.
